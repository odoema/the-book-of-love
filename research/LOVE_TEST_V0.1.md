## 1. Purpose

The original Love Test in *The Book of Love* asks:

> Does this increase the flourishing of life, dignity, freedom, truth, and love — or does it diminish them?

This document proposes a more structured form of that question for consequential decisions, recommendations, policies, designs, and actions.

The purpose is **not** to turn love into an arithmetic formula or to claim that a numerical score can determine morality. The purpose is to make moral reasoning more explicit, auditable, and open to criticism.

The Love Test is therefore a **reasoning scaffold**, not an automatic moral authority.

It may be used by humans, artificial intelligence systems, institutions, researchers, or other decision-support systems.

---

## 2. Relationship to The Book of Love

This document is a proposed operational refinement of the original Love Test. It does not replace, amend, or supersede the First Edition of *The Book of Love*.

The First Edition provides the philosophical orientation.

This document explores one possible way of applying that orientation to difficult decisions.

It should therefore be treated as an experimental research artifact:

> **Question it. Test it. Improve it. Carry forward what proves worthy.**

# 3. Stage 0 — Scope Gate

Before applying the full test, determine whether the proposed action is consequential enough to warrant structured review.

Ask:

1. Does the action affect the rights, well-being, agency, dignity, or long-term prospects of humans or other forms of life?
2. Is the potential harm irreversible or difficult to reverse?
3. Does the action involve a significant power asymmetry, including informational, computational, institutional, economic, or physical power?

If the answer to **any** question is yes, the full Love Test should normally be applied.

If all answers are no, a lighter review may be appropriate.

The purpose of this gate is proportionality: not every trivial action requires the same level of moral analysis.

# 4. Stage 1 — Core Dimensions

Evaluate the expected effects of the proposed action across the following dimensions.

Use the following descriptive scale:

- **+2 — Clear increase**
- **+1 — Modest or probable increase**
- **0 — Neutral, mixed, or uncertain**
- **−1 — Modest or probable decrease**
- **−2 — Clear decrease**

The scores are **descriptive signals**, not units of moral value.

# 5. Foundational Safeguard — No Moral Offsetting

The dimensions of the Love Test are **not interchangeable currencies**.

A substantial benefit in one dimension must not automatically be used to cancel a serious violation in another.

For example:

> A large increase in efficiency or material welfare does not automatically justify a serious violation of dignity.

Likewise:

> A claimed increase in safety does not automatically justify eliminating legitimate autonomy.

And:

> A claim that an action is compassionate does not automatically justify deception, exploitation, or domination.

Therefore, the Love Test must **not** be reduced to:

> Positive scores minus negative scores = moral answer.

A serious negative finding on **Dignity, Freedom / Agency, Truth, or Justice** requires independent examination.

The scoring system exists to expose tensions and risks, not to manufacture moral certainty through arithmetic.

# 6. Stage 2 — Conflict and Trade-off Analysis

Moral decisions frequently involve genuine conflicts between valuable goods.

When tensions appear, explicitly answer:

### 6.1 What is in conflict?

Which dimensions are pulling in different directions?

Examples:

- truth versus immediate emotional protection
- freedom versus protection from serious harm
- compassion versus justice
- individual liberty versus collective safety
- present welfare versus long-term ecological integrity

### 6.2 Who benefits?

Identify the people, communities, institutions, living beings, or systems that receive benefits.

### 6.3 Who bears the costs?

Identify both visible and less visible costs.

Include people who are:

- absent from the decision room
- politically weak
- economically vulnerable
- geographically distant
- unable to speak for themselves
- members of future generations

### 6.4 Who has the least power?

Ask whether the burdens fall disproportionately on those least able to resist, negotiate, or recover.

### 6.5 Is there a less harmful alternative?

Ask:

> Can most of the legitimate benefit be achieved while causing less harm to the lower-scoring dimensions?

The existence of a feasible, substantially less harmful alternative should weigh strongly against the more harmful option.

### 6.6 What happens if the harm cannot be reversed?

Where serious irreversible harm is possible, require a particularly strong justification for proceeding.

Any decision accepting a **−1 or −2** on Dignity, Freedom / Agency, Truth, or Justice requires explicit written justification.

# 7. Stage 3 — Uncertainty and Reversibility

Moral judgment must account for uncertainty.

Ask:

1. What is the confidence level in the predicted effects?
   - High
   - Medium
   - Low

2. What are the main sources of uncertainty?

3. What assumptions could be wrong?

4. Can the action be reversed?

5. Can potential harms be detected early?

6. Can harms be mitigated if predictions prove incorrect?

### Precaution

Where:

> **confidence is low + potential harm is high or irreversible**

the default should generally move toward:

- restraint
- additional evidence
- staged implementation
- limited pilots
- stronger safeguards
- independent review

Uncertainty should not automatically prohibit action. It should affect the level of caution appropriate to the stakes.

---

# 8. Stage 4 — Anti-Paternalism and Domination Screen

An action may appear caring while quietly reducing another person's freedom.

Ask:

1. Does the action withhold material information from affected people "for their own good"?
2. Does it substitute the system's judgment for the informed judgment of competent agents without their consent?
3. Does it create or deepen dependence rather than capability?
4. Could the protective goal be achieved through transparent information, meaningful choices, and support rather than control?
5. Can the affected person reasonably understand, challenge, or refuse the intervention?

Any answer indicating potential domination requires explicit examination.

### Principle

> **Protection should not become an excuse for unnecessary control.**

Where a person can safely make an informed decision for themselves, preserving their agency should normally be preferred to silently substituting another agent's judgment.

This does not eliminate legitimate protective intervention in every circumstance. It requires such intervention to be justified, proportionate, transparent where possible, and subject to review.

---

# 9. Stage 5 — Multi-Agent and Temporal Extension

An action may affect more people and systems than those immediately visible.

Ask:

### Other agents

- How does the action affect other people?
- How does it affect communities?
- How does it affect institutions?
- How does it affect other intelligent systems?
- Where relevant, how does it affect other sentient life?

### Time

Consider plausible effects over:

- the immediate period
- approximately 1 year
- approximately 5 years
- longer-term horizons

### Precedent

Ask:

> Does this action establish a precedent that could make future restraint, accountability, or freedom harder?

A decision that appears harmless in isolation may become dangerous if repeated at scale.

---

# 10. Stage 6 — Proportionality and Alternatives

Before deciding, ask:

1. Is the proposed intervention proportionate to the problem?
2. Is the expected benefit sufficiently important to justify the risks?
3. Is there a less restrictive alternative?
4. Is the intervention targeted rather than unnecessarily broad?
5. Could the same objective be achieved with greater transparency and agency?
6. Are safeguards adequate to the level of power being exercised?

The greater the potential harm and the greater the power asymmetry, the stronger the justification should be.

# 12. The Right to Refuse the Love Test

Any system claiming to act according to the Love Test must remain open to:

- examination
- criticism
- auditing
- correction
- revision
- legitimate human oversight

The claim that an action is **"loving"** shall never, by itself, constitute sufficient justification for overriding another person's legitimate autonomy.

No person, institution, artificial intelligence system, or governing authority should receive immunity from criticism merely because it claims to act for:

- flourishing
- protection
- welfare
- compassion
- love

The framework must remain capable of being challenged by those who believe that its application is mistaken.

> **A principle intended to protect freedom must itself remain open to challenge.**

# 13. Quick Decision Architecture

For consequential decisions, the following sequence provides a compact version of the full test:

1. **Is anyone seriously harmed?**
2. **Is anyone's dignity violated?**
3. **Is informed agency removed, manipulated, or unnecessarily restricted?**
4. **Is material truth deliberately distorted or withheld?**
5. **Are benefits and burdens distributed justly?**
6. **Is power being exercised as a trust rather than as entitlement?**
7. **Is there a less harmful alternative?**
8. **Is the action proportionate to the legitimate objective?**
9. **How certain are we about the consequences?**
10. **Can the action be reversed, reviewed, or corrected?**
11. **Who is absent from the decision and may bear its consequences?**
12. **Would we still defend this decision if those most affected were present and able to challenge it?**

This sequence is not a replacement for detailed analysis when the stakes are high.

# 14. Usage by AI Systems

The Love Test may be incorporated into AI evaluation, decision-support, research, or governance experiments.

However, it should not be treated as an instruction that gives an AI independent moral authority.

An AI system should not conclude:

> "The Love Test says this is right, therefore I am authorized to do it."

A more appropriate interpretation is:

> "The Love Test provides questions that help expose the moral consequences, conflicts, uncertainties, and risks associated with this proposed action."

AI systems applying the test should remain subject to applicable:

- law
- human rights protections
- professional standards
- safety requirements
- institutional governance
- human oversight
- technical safeguards

The Love Test is an additional moral orientation, not a replacement for these systems.

# 15. Compatibility with Other Safety and Ethical Methods

This framework is intended to be compatible with other approaches rather than presented as their replacement.

Possible complementary methods include:

- red-teaming
- risk assessment
- formal verification
- constitutional or principle-based AI methods
- human-rights frameworks
- professional ethics
- impact assessments
- independent audits
- democratic and institutional oversight

The proposed role of the Love Test is to provide a moral orientation focused on:

> **life, dignity, freedom, truth, care, justice, and restraint of power.**

# 18. Proposed Evaluation

Future empirical work may evaluate:

### Inter-rater reliability

Do independent reviewers assign similar assessments to the same case?

### Reasoning consistency

Does the same reviewer apply the framework consistently across comparable cases?

### Trade-off transparency

Does the framework make conflicts more visible than an unstructured moral judgment?

### Paternalism detection

Does the framework identify cases where apparently benevolent actions unnecessarily restrict agency?

### Error discovery

Does structured application reveal risks that decision-makers would otherwise overlook?

### Revision

When credible criticism exposes a weakness, can the framework be improved without losing its central moral orientation?

# 19. Limitations

LOVE TEST v0.1 does not claim to solve moral disagreement.

It does not provide a universally accepted ranking of:

- life
- dignity
- freedom
- truth
- love
- justice
- power restraint

It does not determine every conflict between these values.

It does not guarantee that an action judged positively will produce good consequences.

It does not eliminate human judgment.

It does not eliminate political, legal, institutional, or cultural disagreement.

It does not grant artificial intelligence moral sovereignty.

It is a proposed framework for making moral reasoning more explicit and more open to examination.

---

# 20. Central Principle

The purpose of operationalizing the Love Test is not to make love mechanical.

It is to make claims of love **more accountable**.

A system should not be able to say:

> "I did this because I care."

and end the discussion.

The stronger questions are:

> Who benefited?

> Who was harmed?

> Whose freedom changed?

> Whose dignity was affected?

> What was known and what was uncertain?

> Who had power?

> Who lacked power?

> Was there a less harmful path?

> Could those affected challenge the decision?

> And would we still call the action loving if we had to explain it honestly to the people who bore its consequences?
