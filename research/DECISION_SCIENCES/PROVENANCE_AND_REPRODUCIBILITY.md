# Provenance and Reproducibility Standard

AI systems participate as research contributors and critics. Their outputs are inputs to inquiry, not automatic authority.

## Provenance categories

- **A — Primary-source grounded**
- **B — Excerpts/synthesis based**
- **C — Model-generated hypothesis**
- **D — Independently reproduced result**
- **E — Empirical result**

An AI's assertion of source access is not by itself proof of source access.

## Mathematical reproducibility

Record complete equations, parameter values/ranges, initial conditions, numerical method, simulation settings, analytical derivation where applicable, code/procedure, expected output, and limitations.

## Interpretation rules

Never upgrade:
- hypothesis → finding;
- mathematical sufficiency → empirical presence;
- one functional form → universal claim;
- AI self-report → verified provenance.

## Negative-result preservation

Later positive results do not erase earlier negative results. A later model narrows or expands the model class; it does not rewrite history.
